## Editing in Figma

- Drag the files onto the canvas, or use File → Import. Each comes in as a 1080 × 1350 group with named layers (`headline`, `body`, `calories-phone` and so on).
- Fonts are **Inter** and **Inter Tight**. Both are Google Fonts, so Figma already has them.
- Each line of text sits at its own position, so if you rewrite a headline you may need to move the line breaks by hand.
- The phones are grouped, with the screen clipped by a mask, so you can move or scale one as a single piece.
- The app icon on slide 8 is embedded as an image.
- Export each slide as PNG at 1x (1080 × 1350).

Colours are the site's: ink `#0a0a0a`, muted `#6e6e73`, faint `#a1a1a6`, surface `#f2f2f7`, night `#0b0b0c`, green `#34c759`, blue `#007aff`, orange `#ff9500`, and the tints `#e3f7e8`, `#dbecff` and `#ffefd9`.

## Alt text

1. "Roughly right every day beats perfectly right for a week." Macro, the calorie and protein tracker for being mindful, not meticulous.
2. Two month calendars. "Perfectly right": seven days logged to the calorie, then three empty weeks. "Roughly right": all 28 days logged, most on target and a few over.
3. "612 kcal" crossed out, and "About 600." beneath it. Counting every calorie to the last digit is stressful; a ballpark logged every day is easier on your head.
4. "Nothing to scan, search, weigh or snap." Crossed out: barcode scanning, food photos, food databases, kitchen scales, serving sizes, ingredient lists.
5. "Two drags and you're done." Two iPhone screens: dragging to 520 calories, then to 38 grams of protein.
6. "14 steps vs. 2." The same homemade spag bol takes 14 steps in a typical tracker and 2 in Macro.
7. "Free forever. And you're not the product." No ads, no trackers, no account, no server. Your meals stay on your iPhone and in your own iCloud.
8. The Macro app icon. "Track your food without the faff." Free forever on iPhone. Join the beta, link in bio.
